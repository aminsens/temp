# Ecological Momentary Assessment in Body-Mounted Accelerometry Studies of Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour

## Search and screening workflow

**Search and screening summary**  
Searches were conducted on April 7, 2026 (Europe/Copenhagen). The search emphasized *concurrent* collection of: (a) body-mounted accelerometer-derived movement behavior and (b) prompted in-the-moment (or near-real-time) participant self-report (EMA / experience sampling / ambulatory self-report) during the same monitoring period. The highest-yield search patterns were combinations of: “ecological momentary assessment” AND “accelerometer” AND (“physical activity” OR “sedentary”), plus device- and design-linked terms (eg, “signal-contingent”, “event-based”, “triggered”, “prompted”, “movisens”, “ActiGraph”, “activPAL”, “Axivity”). citeturn8view0turn15view1turn35view0turn22view1turn15view3

The resulting candidate pool contained three broad clusters:  
- (i) **Feasibility/validity studies** where EMA “current activity” is compared to accelerometer windows around prompts. citeturn8view0turn15view1turn18view2  
- (ii) **Context/determinant studies** where EMA captures affect, motivation, location, social setting, etc., linked to recent or subsequent accelerometer-derived behavior. citeturn24view0turn11view4turn9view3  
- (iii) **Event-triggered / sensor-triggered EMA studies** designed to sample experiences *during specific behavior states* (especially prolonged sitting). citeturn37view1turn37view2  

A practical constraint in this run was that multiple PubMed Central pages were blocked by anti-bot checks, so eligible studies were preferentially drawn from publisher-hosted full text (eg, JMIR, Frontiers, Springer, MDPI) or directly accessible PDFs. This affected breadth more than the conclusions because core “accelerometer + EMA” exemplars in PA/SB research are available through those publishers. citeturn8view0turn15view1turn22view1turn17view0turn15view3

**Inclusion/exclusion logic**  
Inclusion required *all* of the following in the same study period:  
- **Primary movement data** from a **dedicated body-mounted accelerometer** (eg, ActiGraph models, activPAL, Axivity AX3, movisens Move 3) rather than phone-based sensing. citeturn26view1turn25view2turn24view0turn19view0  
- Study focus on **physical activity and/or sedentary behavior** (including posture/movement behavior and free-living behaviour assessment). citeturn8view0turn15view1turn11view0turn12view5turn19view0  
- **EMA (or near-equivalent)** prompted self-report collected repeatedly during monitoring (signal-, interval-, or event-triggered). citeturn26view5turn25view2turn24view0turn37view2turn36view1  

Key exclusions (and how they were handled)  
- **Smartphone-as-sensor** movement measurement: excluded by design (even if EMA was excellent). citeturn8view0turn15view3  
- **Smartwatch/consumer tracker-only sensing**: strongly de-emphasized; a few highly relevant event-triggered EMA methods exist in this space but were treated as *adjacent/context-only* because the scope requested research-grade body-mounted accelerometers as the movement source. citeturn6view0turn33view0  
- Studies that collected accelerometry but did **not** actually use accelerometer-derived behaviors in the analysis (ie, accelerometer present but not driving outcomes) were treated as *adjacent/context-only*, because the core question is EMA utility *within accelerometer-based PA/SB studies*. citeturn40view0

## Executive synthesis

**Executive summary**  
Across the included accelerometer-focused PA/SB studies, EMA is rarely treated as “the” truth about movement. Instead, it functions as a *situational annotation layer*—most often to (a) name the behavior (what kind of activity/sedentary behavior is happening), (b) add context (where/with whom/why/how it feels), and/or (c) provide a time anchor for extracting and interpreting accelerometer windows around prompts. citeturn26view5turn25view2turn9view3turn12view3turn24view0

A consistent pattern is that **EMA’s value increases when accelerometer ambiguity is highest**:  
- Accelerometers capture *movement intensity* well, but not *behavioral domain* (eg, sitting at work vs sitting at home; walking for transport vs walking for leisure). citeturn12view3turn37view1turn10view4  
- Hip/wrist accelerometers struggle with **posture truth**; thigh-worn posture-capable monitors plus event-triggering are repeatedly used when the research question is explicitly about “sitting/lying vs upright” and prolonged sedentary bouts. citeturn25view3turn19view4turn37view2  

Within the strict scope, the literature supporting **EMA as a direct labeling tool for activity classification/HAR model training is thin**; most included studies use EMA for validation, context, or determinant modeling rather than to build supervised classifiers. citeturn18view2turn26view5turn24view0turn37view3

**What EMA contributes beyond body-mounted accelerometers**  
The included studies show four recurring “incremental contributions” beyond what accelerometers alone provide:

1. **Behavioral disambiguation (what exactly is this sedentary time / activity?)**  
Binary or categorical EMA items (eg, “TV/videos/video games” vs sport) can explain *why* two accelerometer windows with similar intensity look different behaviorally, and can validate that “sedentary counts” correspond to the targeted behavior domain (eg, screen-based sedentary behavior). citeturn10view3turn10view5turn12view3  

2. **Contextualization (where/with whom/what situation)**  
In-field “what/where/who” and similar context batteries are repeatedly used to attach interpretable context to accelerometer-derived movement windows and to study context-behavior coupling. citeturn9view3turn12view3turn11view4  

3. **Subjective state capture as a mechanistic layer (affect, fatigue, intention, self-efficacy)**  
EMA enables within-person mapping between momentary states and subsequent accelerometer-derived behavior, supporting time-varying determinant models and the logic for just-in-time interventions (even if causality remains uncertain). citeturn24view0turn22view1turn11view3  

4. **Episode-targeted sampling (especially for prolonged sitting)**  
Event-triggered designs can drastically increase the *efficiency* of collecting contextual data for the episodes that matter (eg, ≥20 min sitting bouts), compared with random prompts that often land in uninformative moments. citeturn37view1turn19view3  

**How EMA is used in PA/SB studies**  
In this narrow accelerometer-centric literature, EMA is most faithfully described as a combination of:  
- **Contextual metadata** (location/social domain/activity type), citeturn9view3turn12view3turn37view3  
- **A behavioral interpretation layer** (especially for sedentary behavior domains and purposeful activity), citeturn10view3turn12view3turn37view1  
- **A time anchor for windowing** (prompt-based extraction of accelerometer features/outcomes), citeturn26view4turn11view0turn24view0  
- **A feasibility/compliance probe** (response rate, non-response bias by behavior state), citeturn26view5turn9view0turn25view4  
- **Occasion-level “near-ground truth”** for *coarse* behavior categories (eg, sitting vs not; screen vs not) when paired with posture-capable sensors or carefully chosen windows—*but not strong ground truth for fine-grained intensity or multi-class activity labeling*. citeturn25view3turn12view4turn18view0  

This role differs sharply from:  
- **Passive sensing** (accelerometers are dense and objective but context-poor), citeturn8view0turn34view4  
- **Retrospective questionnaires** (low temporal resolution; recall bias), citeturn18view0turn25view4  
- **Time-use diaries/activity records** (potentially rich but burdensome; higher recall windows), where EMA-as-designed tries to preserve ecological validity without full diary burden. citeturn36view1turn18view5  
- **Direct observation/video** (high-fidelity labeling but rarely scalable to multi-day free-living), which is why many included studies interpret EMA as “good enough” context rather than definitive ground truth. citeturn37view0turn12view4  

## Role of EMA in validation, labeling, and modeling

**Role of EMA in labelling, validation, and algorithm development**  

*Validation (strongest and most consistent use in included studies)*  
Multiple included studies explicitly tested criterion/construct validity by comparing EMA-reported behavior categories to accelerometer-derived movement in a defined window around prompts (±15 min; 30 min; 2-hour windows). citeturn26view4turn25view4turn10view5turn18view2  
- This approach treats EMA as an *occasion-level indicator* of what the participant believes they were doing, then checks whether accelerometer signals behave as expected (higher sedentary minutes around “sitting/TV”; higher MVPA around “PA/exercise”). citeturn26view4turn10view4turn25view4  
- However, when EMA tries to classify intensity levels (light vs moderate vs vigorous), misclassification becomes obvious; intensity labels are often noisy without careful item design and/or activity-type prompts. citeturn18view0turn18view2  

*Labeling for activity classification / HAR (thin within strict scope)*  
Within the included set, EMA is generally **not** used as a direct multi-class labeling pipeline to train accelerometer-based activity recognition models. Instead:  
- It is used as **weak labels for coarse states** (eg, sedentary presence/absence around prompt), where agreement may be moderate and specificity for “not sedentary” can be poor. citeturn12view4turn9view5  
- It is used to **target moments worth labeling** (event-triggered EMA during prolonged sitting) rather than to generate dense labels. citeturn37view1turn19view3  

*Determinant and predictive modeling (a clear “EMA-as-predictor” pattern)*  
A distinct family of accelerometer + EMA studies uses EMA to measure time-varying determinants (affect, fatigue, intention, self-efficacy), then models accelerometer-derived PA in the minutes/hours after prompts. citeturn22view1turn24view0  
This usage positions EMA as “in-the-moment latent state measurement” rather than behavioral labeling, and the accelerometer as the primary outcome stream. citeturn22view1turn24view0  

**Common EMA variables collected**  
Across included studies, EMA items cluster into a small number of recurring variable families:

- **Current activity / activity type** (eg, “main activity right before the beep”; sport/exercise; screen behaviors). citeturn26view5turn10view3turn18view5  
- **Sedentary behavior domain and posture proxies** (eg, sitting/lying; TV/computer; “domain of sedentary activity”). citeturn25view2turn37view3turn12view3  
- **Location and social context** (“where”; “who with”; alone vs not). citeturn9view3turn12view3turn11view3turn37view3  
- **Affect and feelings** (positive/negative affect; irritation; feeling down), sometimes paired with **fatigue/pain** as physical feeling states. citeturn11view3turn24view0turn6view0  
- **Motivational/cognitive determinants** (intention; self-efficacy) when the goal is to understand dynamic predictors of activity. citeturn24view0turn22view1  
- **Domain-coded reporting frameworks** (transport, occupational, exercise, sedentary) used for validity comparisons and coarse-grained interpretation at scale. citeturn36view1turn36view4  

From an “operational strength” perspective in this literature:  
- The most **common + operationally strong** variables are *what/where/who* and coarse activity categories (low ambiguity, fast to answer). citeturn9view3turn12view3  
- The most **scientifically attractive but operationally fragile** variables are fine-grained **intensity self-classifications** (light vs moderate), which show poor agreement with accelerometer-derived intensity distributions in at least one validation study. citeturn18view0turn18view2  
- Determinant items (affect, intention, self-efficacy) are feasible and analytically valuable when the study is explicitly designed for within-person modeling, with careful temporal window selection after prompts. citeturn24view0turn22view1  

## Methodological linkage challenges and blind spots

**Methodological challenges linking EMA to accelerometer data**  
The included literature surfaces a relatively consistent set of alignment problems:

- **Prompt–response lag**: Even “momentary” items are typically answered minutes after the signal. One adolescent study reported a mean ~5 minutes between prompt signal and answering, which matters if the accelerometer window is short or if behavior changes rapidly. citeturn11view3  
- **Recall-window mismatch**: Studies using “past 2 hours” or “past 3 hours” items must match longer accelerometer windows, and inconsistencies in recall framing can create predictable mismatches (eg, a prompt that changes to “since waking” undermines a 2-hour matching design). citeturn10view3turn10view5turn18view5  
- **Event duration mismatch**: A prompt captures a moment; the accelerometer captures continuous behavior. Decisions like ±15 minutes, prior 30 minutes, or prior 5 minutes are not interchangeable and can change validity conclusions. citeturn26view4turn11view0turn12view4turn25view3  
- **Sparse EMA vs dense time series**: Most time-based protocols generate <10 labels/day, leaving most accelerometer time unlabeled; this supports interpretation and within-person modeling, but is inherently weak supervision for full-day activity classification. citeturn9view0turn24view0turn19view3  
- **Missingness that depends on behavior**: Missed prompts can occur *because the participant is active* (or because the phone is elsewhere), introducing missing-not-at-random bias. This was explicitly tested in adults, with evidence that unanswered prompts can coincide with higher MVPA for some subgroups. citeturn8view0turn26view5  
- **Device nonwear and synchronization failures**: Both classic nonwear (accelerometer not worn) and modern technical coupling issues (eg, BLE disconnections in triggered designs) reduce the number of “matched” EMA–accelerometer pairs. citeturn26view0turn37view1turn37view4  

**Alignment strategies actually used (and which appear strongest)**  
1. **Fixed windowing around prompts (±15 min; pre-30 min; pre-5 min)**  
This is the dominant approach for feasibility/validity studies and for linking recent behavior to current-report items. It is strongest when the EMA question is truly “current activity right before beep” and when analysis windows are chosen to absorb response delay. citeturn26view4turn25view4turn11view0turn12view4  

2. **Longer recall windows paired to longer accelerometer aggregates (2-hour blocks)**  
Useful when the EMA item asks about the past X hours; but vulnerable to inconsistent item framing and to “averaging away” the behavior of interest. citeturn10view5turn10view3  

3. **Event-triggered sampling using posture-capable sensors**  
This is the most targeted strategy for sedentary behavior episode interpretation because it improves the chance that an EMA response corresponds to a truly relevant bout (eg, ≥20 minutes sitting), not an arbitrary time slice. The trade-off is technical complexity and the need for robust time-out rules to control burden. citeturn37view1turn37view3turn19view3  

4. **Post-trigger outcome windows (15–120 minutes after EMA)**  
This strategy is central when EMA measures determinants and accelerometer-derived PA is treated as a subsequent outcome; it is conceptually clean for prediction/JITAI targeting but still vulnerable to confounding (bidirectionality) and requires careful handling of overlapping windows across prompts. citeturn22view1turn24view0  

**Common weaknesses and blind spots**  
Within included studies (and reinforced by PA/SB EMA methodological reviews), several recurring gaps stand out:

- **EMA collected but not used to resolve accelerometer ambiguity**: Some studies use EMA mostly for feasibility/compliance or for broad validation, but do not leverage it to meaningfully decompose sedentary time into domains or to explain why misclassification happens. citeturn9view0turn35view0  
- **Limited treatment of “other” responses and label noise**: When context menus are offered, “other” can become unclassifiable and is often excluded, shrinking usable linked data and potentially biasing context distributions. citeturn9view5turn12view3  
- **Weak reporting of content validity / item development**: A dedicated systematic review on EMA in PA/SB highlights the content-validity problem (items often used without strong validity justification), suggesting a field-level need for more rigorous item development and reporting. citeturn38search6  
- **Underuse of posture-capable sensing when SB is the target**: Hip/wrist accelerometers are still common even when the construct of interest is “sedentary time” (which is posture-anchored). Included studies explicitly note that hip accelerometers cannot distinguish sitting vs standing and point to inclinometer/thigh-based monitors as the solution. citeturn11view1turn19view4turn25view3  
- **Overburden risks and reactivity**: Multiple studies and participant feedback indicate that frequent prompting can affect behavior or compliance; one older-adult study detected small post-prompt PA changes, and protocol-focused work notes that high-burden reporting can alter the behavior being measured. citeturn25view4turn33view0  

## Best-practice recommendations and representative exemplars

**Best-practice recommendations (ideal EMA component in accelerometer PA/SB studies)**  
These recommendations are grounded primarily in the included study designs (and supported by PA/SB EMA methodological guidance where noted).

**If the goal is physical activity classification (multi-class activity recognition)**  
- Prioritize **behavior type** over intensity labels (eg, “walking for transport”, “leisure walking”, “household chores”, “sitting at desk”, “screen time”) because intensity self-classifications are demonstrably noisy. citeturn18view0turn12view3turn10view3  
- Treat EMA as **weak supervision**: use it to label *selected windows* (eg, the 2–10 minutes around response time) and propagate labels cautiously (probabilistic labels, confidence weights). The included record base shows that sparse prompts and response lags make dense labeling unrealistic without event-triggering. citeturn11view3turn12view4turn19view3  
- Add **event-triggered prompts** for rare/high-value activities (eg, sustained walking, cycling) only if triggering is reliable and burden is capped via time-outs; otherwise, random prompts will under-sample those episodes. citeturn26view5turn37view1  

**If the goal is sedentary behavior interpretation**  
- Use **thigh-worn posture-capable monitors** (or equivalent) when “sitting/lying vs standing” is central; hip accelerometers alone are insufficient for posture truth. citeturn25view3turn11view1turn19view4  
- Use **sedentary-triggered EMA** to sample contexts of prolonged sitting (eg, ≥20 minutes), because this design explicitly maximizes “context-per-sedentary-bout” yield. citeturn37view1turn37view3  
- Keep the EMA battery short and context-focused: (domain + location + social company) showed feasibility and relevance in sedentary-triggered and random-prompt studies. citeturn37view3turn12view3turn9view3  

**If the goal is context-aware behavior analysis (mechanisms, determinants, within-person dynamics)**  
- Use a **time-based random-within-window schedule** (eg, 6/day) to reduce systematic time-of-day bias and to support within-person inference. citeturn24view0turn25view2turn9view0  
- Explicitly model (or at least report) **prompt-response latency**, and design analytic windows that account for it. citeturn11view3turn24view0  
- If the objective is prediction/JITAI logic, predefine **post-trigger outcome windows** (eg, 15/30/60/120 min) and handle overlap carefully. citeturn22view1turn24view0  

**If the goal is multimodal algorithm development (accelerometer + EMA features)**  
- Use EMA primarily as:  
  - (i) **contextual features** (where/with whom/domain), and/or  
  - (ii) **weak labels** for a targeted subset of windows (especially event-triggered),  
  rather than a blanket “ground truth” label stream. citeturn12view4turn37view3turn18view0  
- For training data efficiency, adopt a **hybrid sampling design** (random prompts for background coverage + event-triggered prompts for episodes of interest) with strict burden caps. The sedentary-triggered literature provides concrete time-out mechanisms and accuracy evaluation logic for triggers. citeturn37view1turn19view3  
- Follow reporting standards tailored to EMA (eg, checklist-style reporting of schedule, prompting rules, compliance, missingness) to improve reproducibility and comparability across datasets. citeturn38search1turn38search6  

**What should be avoided**  
- Using fine-grained self-reported intensity categories as “truth labels” without calibration/validation; a validation study shows that light/moderate distinctions are especially unreliable. citeturn18view0turn18view2  
- Inconsistent recall frames within the same protocol (eg, mixing “past 2 hours” with “since waking”) because it creates structural disagreement with time-matched accelerometer windows. citeturn10view3turn10view5  
- Designing an event-triggered system without robust nonwear/connectivity handling and without time-out rules; technical instability and burden control are decisive for usable linked data. citeturn37view4turn37view1  

**Representative studies and datasets (strict-scope exemplars)**  

- *Prompt-window validation archetype (adults)*: Frontiers in Psychology 2012 feasibility/validity study  
  Why it matters: establishes the “EMA as time-matched activity-type anchor” pattern, including tests of non-response bias and potential prompt reactivity using ±15-minute accelerometer windows. citeturn8view0turn26view4turn26view5  
  Modeling consequence: supports EMA as a feasible occasion-level classifier for broad activity types, but also documents missingness patterns linked to activity. citeturn26view5turn8view0  

- *Posture-capable pairing (older adults)*: Frontiers in Psychology 2018 older adult study  
  Why it matters: uses a thigh-worn posture-capable monitor and a structured random prompt schedule, showing high compliance and providing a concrete example of posture-aware validation and reactivity testing. citeturn25view2turn25view3turn25view4  
  Modeling consequence: demonstrates how posture-aware accelerometry changes what “validation” means for SB, compared to hip counts. citeturn25view3turn11view1  

- *Minimal context triad feasibility (students)*: JMIR Public Health and Surveillance 2017 MovingU study  
  Why it matters: operationalizes a small, high-yield context battery (“what/where/who”) alongside wrist accelerometry and quantifies usable EMA thresholds and compliance tiers. citeturn9view0turn9view3turn9view2  
  Modeling consequence: highlights that missing EMA is non-trivial even in motivated samples and should be planned for analytically. citeturn9view0  

- *Child screen-time disambiguation*: JMIR mHealth and uHealth 2018 child validation study  
  Why it matters: shows that EMA can disambiguate the *type* of sedentary behavior (screen), not just “low counts,” and quantifies associations in matched 2-hour windows. citeturn10view0turn10view5  
  Modeling consequence: supports using EMA to label specific sedentary domains that accelerometers alone cannot identify. citeturn10view3turn10view4  

- *Within-person determinant modeling*: JMIR Aging 2023 older adult study  
  Why it matters: cleanly demonstrates the “EMA determinants → subsequent accelerometer behavior” pipeline with multiple post-trigger windows, aligning with JITAI logic. citeturn24view0turn22view1  
  Modeling consequence: EMA can function as a time-varying feature stream for predictive models, but causal direction remains unresolved without stronger designs. citeturn22view1turn24view0  

- *Event-triggered sedentary context system*: JMIR mHealth and uHealth 2020 sedentary-triggered EMA development and feasibility  
  Why it matters: provides a practical blueprint for sedentary-episode targeting (trigger thresholds, time-outs, prompt logic, synchronization pipeline) and quantifies trigger “accuracy” compared with random prompting simulations. citeturn37view1turn19view3turn37view3  
  Modeling consequence: best available strict-scope exemplar for building context-rich sedentary datasets and for designing event-based labeling streams (with technical caveats). citeturn37view4turn37view3  

**Final synthesis: what everyone does, does not do, and should do**  

What everyone does (in the included accelerometer+EMA PA/SB literature)  
- Uses EMA to add **semantic meaning and context** to accelerometer windows: “what activity,” “where,” “with whom,” and (in determinant studies) “how I feel / what I intend.” citeturn26view5turn9view3turn12view3turn24view0  
- Links EMA to accelerometry through **explicit windowing rules** (±15 min; prior 30 min; prior 5 min; 2-hour blocks; 15–120 min post-trigger), and usually excludes nonwear-linked prompts. citeturn26view4turn11view0turn12view4turn25view3turn24view0  
- Treats EMA primarily as **validation and interpretation**, not as dense labels for activity recognition model training. citeturn18view2turn26view5turn24view0  

What everyone does not do (or rarely does)  
- Rarely builds **scalable, high-fidelity labeled accelerometer datasets** using EMA alone as the label source; EMA sparsity, response lag, and self-report ambiguity make it weak as standalone “ground truth.” citeturn18view0turn11view3turn19view0  
- Often under-specifies or under-exploits **content validity** and **item development**, a weakness identified in systematic methodological work on EMA for PA/SB. citeturn38search6turn38search1  
- Frequently lacks robust solutions for **missing-not-at-random EMA** (eg, missingness correlated with MVPA), which can bias inferences if untreated. citeturn26view5turn8view0  

What everyone should do (if the field wants more scientific and modeling value per unit burden)  
- Decide explicitly whether EMA is intended to be: **context metadata**, **weak labels**, **determinant predictors**, or **compliance/quality signals**—and then design prompts, recall frames, and matching windows accordingly. citeturn24view0turn26view4turn37view3  
- For sedentary research, prioritize **posture-capable body placement** and consider **event-triggered EMA** for prolonged sitting to maximize relevance and interpretability of collected self-report. citeturn25view3turn37view1turn19view4  
- For activity classification ambitions, treat EMA-based labels as **probabilistic/weak supervision** and use hybrid sampling (random + event-based) to reduce selection bias and under-sampling of important activity classes. citeturn26view5turn37view1turn12view4  
- Report EMA design and outcomes using checklist-style conventions (schedule, delivery, response window, compliance, missingness handling, linkage window) to enable reproducibility and cross-study comparability. citeturn38search1turn38search6
